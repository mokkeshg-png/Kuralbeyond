3.	Executive	Summary
TamilPalam	is	a	gamified,	research-backed	Tamil	language	learning	platform	designed	for	English-speaking	children	living	outside	Tamil	Nadu
who	have	little	or	no	prior	Tamil	knowledge.	The	platform	uses	
Tanglish	(Tamil	written	in	English	letters)	as	a	temporary	learning	bridge
,
progressively	reducing	transliteration	support	as	learners	gain	mastery	of	Tamil	script,	vocabulary,	sentences,	and	reading	comprehension.
The	product	addresses	a	critical	gap:	existing	Tamil	learning	apps	for	children	abroad	either	assume	prior	script	knowledge,	lack	progressive
transliteration	reduction,	or	provide	insufficient	audio-first	instruction.	TamilPalam	differentiates	through	its	
Progressive	Tanglish	Reduction
system	—	a	structured	five-stage	pathway	that	moves	learners	from	full	Tanglish	support	to	independent	Tamil	reading.
Core	deliverables	(P0):
	English/Tamil	interface	localization,	onboarding	and	assessment,	level-based	curriculum	(Levels	0–7),
letter/word/sentence	learning,	listening	and	speaking	practice,	quizzes,	persistent	progress,	gamification,	and	responsive	design.
Differentiating	features	(P1):
	Progressive	Tanglish	Reduction,	personalized	revision,	real-life	Tamil	missions,	audio-first	learning,	adaptive
recommendations,	and	cultural	storytelling.
Future	expansion	(P2):
	Tamil	Research	Assistant	for	literary	content	explanation.

4.	Product	Vision
Build	a	polished,	interactive,	gamified	Tamil	learning	platform	that	helps	an	English-speaking	beginner	progress	from	recognizing	Tamil	letters	to
understanding	words,	reading	sentences,	participating	in	conversations,	and	reading	simple	paragraphs	and	stories.
Tanglish	is	the	initial	bridge	between	English	and	Tamil.	The	product	must	
not	stop	at	teaching	Tanglish	spellings
.	Its	educational	objective	is	to
progressively	reduce	dependence	on	Tanglish	and	help	learners	recognize	and	read	Tamil	script	independently.
The	experience	should	feel	like	a	thoughtfully	designed	educational	product	created	by	a	premium	product-design	team	—	not	a	generic	AI-
generated	website.
5.	Problem	Statement
P10	—	How	Can	Tamil	Be	Taught	to	Tamil	Children	Living	Abroad?
Children	living	abroad	who	do	not	speak	Tamil	at	home	need	an	engaging	online	way	to	learn	Tamil	and	continue	learning	consistently.	The
learning	platform	should	encourage	their	interest	and	measure	their	learning	progress.
The	solution	must	specifically	address:
1.	
How	to	teach	Tamil	to	children	who	have	little	or	no	prior	Tamil	knowledge.
2.	
How	to	make	learning	interesting	and	sustainable.
3.	
How	to	measure	learning	progress.
4.	
How	to	help	learners	gradually	develop	real	Tamil	reading,	writing,	listening,	and	speaking	skills.
The	learner	knows	English	and	is	learning	Tamil.	The	problem	is	
not
	teaching	English	to	Tamil	speakers.

21.	Tamil-Script	Learning	Model
Unicode	Foundation
Tamil	script	in	Unicode	occupies	code	points	U+0B80–U+0BFF.	Independent	vowels	(Uyir)	occupy	U+0B85–U+0B94;	consonants	(Mei)	occupy
U+0B95–U+0BB9.	Vowel	signs	(Uyirmei)	are	combining	characters	that	modify	consonants.	The	virama	(U+0BCD,	்)	suppresses	the	inherent
vowel.
Critical	implementation	note:
	Tamil	is	a	complex	script	requiring	proper	rendering	support.	Browsers	and	OS	font	stacks	must	handle	combining
characters,	ligatures	(e.g.,	
R),	
and	the	split	vowel	sign	ொ	(U+0BCA).	Use	Unicode	NFC	normalization	for	all	stored	and	displayed	text.	Test
rendering	in	Chrome,	Safari,	Firefox,	and	Edge	on	Windows,	macOS,	iOS,	and	Android.

22.	Tanglish	Transliteration	Model
